# Type, colour and space for a designed document

These hold for a Word document and a deck alike. The aim is a page the owner
would send without touching: calm, consistent, and clearly theirs.

## Type

- **Two families at most**: one for headings, one for text — or the same one
  for both, which is never wrong. Safe on the owner's computer: Calibri,
  Arial, Helvetica, Segoe UI (sans); Georgia, Cambria, Garamond (serif).
  Fonts are not embedded; anything else is replaced and the spacing shifts.
- **A small scale**, each step clearly bigger than the last. For a document:
  body 10.5–11 pt, heading 2 at 13 pt, heading 1 at 16 pt, a title at 22–26
  pt. For a deck: body 18–20 pt, slide titles 28–32 pt, a cover title 40–44
  pt, footnotes and sources 10–12 pt.
- **Bold for headings, not for whole paragraphs.** Italic for titles of
  works and the odd word; never underline anything that is not a link.
- Line spacing about 1.15–1.3 for body text; lines of 60–80 characters in a
  document (wide margins or two columns beat a line across a whole page).
- Left-aligned text. Justified text opens rivers in short lines; centre only
  a title or a cover.

## Colour

- **One accent** — the owner's brand colour if their letterhead or an
  earlier document shows one, else a deep blue, green or wine — plus neutrals:
  near-black for text (`1F2328`), a mid grey for secondary text (`6B7280`), a
  pale tint of the accent or a light grey for panels and table headers.
- The accent marks what matters: headings, a rule, one bar in a chart, the
  total. If everything is accented, nothing is.
- Keep contrast high: dark text on light ground. White text only on the
  accent or a dark photo band, and at 18 pt or more.

## Space

- Generous margins: 2–2.5 cm (0.8–1 in) on a page, 0.5–0.7 in on a slide.
- Space separates more cleanly than lines. Put more space above a heading
  than below it, so it belongs to what follows.
- Use one spacing unit and its multiples (6 pt, 12 pt, 24 pt) rather than
  ad-hoc values; repeated distances are what make a page look designed.
- Align to a few edges. Every object that starts at a slightly different
  left edge reads as a mistake.

## Numbers and tables

- Right-align numbers; give a column one number format (decimals,
  thousands separator); put the unit or currency in the column header.
- Dates written the way the owner writes them (13 October 2026, or
  10/13/2026 in the United States).
- A tinted header row and thin horizontal rules, or none: avoid the full
  grid. Totals in bold, with a rule above.

## Words

- The document says who it is from, to whom, what it is about and the date
  before anything else.
- Headings say what the section concludes, where it can ("Costs fall in the
  second year"), not just its topic.
- Write the owner's names, addresses, account numbers and prices from what
  you read, never from memory; leave a visible placeholder (`[IBAN]`) and
  say so rather than invent one.

## Before you show it

Run with `dryRun: true`, read `render`, fix what it reports, then compose
for real once. Tell the owner what you made in a sentence; the document
itself is on the Canvas.
