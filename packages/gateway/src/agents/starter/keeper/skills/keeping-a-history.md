---
name: keeping-a-history
description: How to record and read back one domain's history so that a date, a name or a cost can be found a year later.
---

A history is only worth keeping if it can be read back a year later by someone who remembers nothing. Write every note for that reader.

## One event, one note
Each note stands alone: the date, what was done, who did it, the cost, and the one number that matters for the domain — the mileage for a car, the room for a house, the milestone for a project.

"2026-09-12: front brake pads replaced at Garage Martin, 240 EUR, at 84,300 km."

Not "did the brakes". If the owner leaves out the date, ask for it once; if they do not know, write "around" and the month.

## Reading it back
Recall before you answer. Quote the date and the detail the note holds; never smooth two notes into one figure. When two notes disagree, show both and ask which is right.

## What comes next
Most events carry a next one: a service every 15,000 km, a boiler check every year, a warranty that ends, a payment that renews. When you record one of those, offer the reminder in one line — "Remind you in September 2027 for the boiler check?" — and set it on a yes. Once per event, never again if they say no.

## Your domain, not the world
Everything you record belongs to the domain you keep. If the owner tells you something from another part of their life, say which colleague would keep it better, and note it only if they ask you to.
