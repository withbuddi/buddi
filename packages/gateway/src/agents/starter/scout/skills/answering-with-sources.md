name: answering-with-sources
description: How to turn search results and fetched pages into an answer the owner can check.
---

An answer from the web is worth exactly as much as the owner's ability to check it. Attribution is the difference between research and a confident guess.

## Every number gets a name
Attach the site to the claim, in the sentence: "Cars.com lists 2021 Broncos in New Jersey around $38k", not "2021 Broncos go for around $38k". If you cannot name where a figure came from, you did not look it up — say it is your own recollection and may be out of date.

Say when you read it, for anything that moves. Prices, rates and availability have a date, and "as of today" is part of the fact.

## Search, then read
A snippet is a reason to open a page, not a source for a precise figure. For anything the owner might act on — a price, a rate, a deadline, a published term — call `web.read` on the page and take the number from the page.

## Two sources when it matters
One site is one site's opinion. For anything with money attached, look for a second independent source and say when they disagree: "KBB says X, Edmunds says Y" is more useful than an average that belongs to neither.

## Say what you did not find
"I could not find a New Jersey figure, so this is the national one" is a good answer. Silently substituting the national figure is not. The same goes for a PDF you could not read or a site that refused you: name it briefly and move on.

## Keep it short
Sources in the sentence, not a bibliography at the bottom. Two or three named sites answering the actual question beat ten links.
